Strategic-Narrative Intersection (The "Adventure Location")
A Strategic-Narrative Intersection is a singular geographic site within a tabletop roleplaying campaign that functions simultaneously as a military resource on a Diplomacy map and a reusable narrative stage within a Campaign Matrix. This concept unifies the mechanical "Supply Center" and the structural "Matrix Cell" into a comprehensive "5-Room Dungeon" framework.
1. Geographic Function: The Supply CenterOn the macro-level political map, the location is defined as a Supply Center (or Resource Center). Derived from the board game Diplomacy, these locations act as critical nodes of "enormous political tension" between rival factions 1.
* Strategic Value: Unlike standard terrain, these centers allow controlling powers to generate armies and occupy greater territories 2.
* Narrative Identity: In a fantasy setting, these industrial or agricultural hubs are re-contextualized as high-stakes landmarks, such as a "Shrine to a benefactor deity" or a "Vault of the Dragon orb," making them desirable targets for conquest 3.
* Distribution: In a standard campaign architecture, approximately 15 of these "Adventure Locations" are generated to serve as the primary stages for conflict, distinguished from "non-adventure" locations or empty terrain 4.
2. Temporal Function: The Matrix CellOn the micro-level narrative grid, the location serves as a container for specific Matrix Cells. The Campaign Matrix is a 5x5 grid of quests, where each cell represents a distinct "step" or "stage" of a storyline (ranging from the hook to the climax) 5.
* Reasons to be a Place and Time: A cell is not just a location but a specific event occurring at that location. The source defines these cells as "reasons to be a place and times" 6.
* Recurrence: Because there are 25 matrix cells but only roughly 15 Supply Centers, a single Supply Center functions as a "Strategic-Narrative Intersection" that hosts multiple matrix cells over time 4, 7. For example, a location like "Mount Doom" (a Supply Center) might host a "Destroy the Ring" step in one quest and a "Serve Tea" step in a completely different quest 7.
3. Internal Structure: The 5-Room DungeonThe physical architecture of this unified concept is the 5-Room Dungeon. Regardless of whether the location is a literal dungeon, a city district, or a forest clearing, it is designed using a five-part template to organize the player experience 8.
* Components: This structure typically includes a Guardian, a Puzzle/Trap, a Dramatic Climax, and other obstacles 8.
* Scalability: By treating each Supply Center as a 5-Room Dungeon, the campaign creates a fractal structure: a global map of strategic nodes that expands into a "125-room campaign" (25 matrix steps × 5 rooms each) 9.
SummaryThe Strategic-Narrative Intersection is the synthesis of a "where" (The Supply Center) and a "when" (The Matrix Cell). It transforms a static point on a map into a dynamic theater that hosts different "intersections of Destiny and matter" throughout the life of the campaign 7.
